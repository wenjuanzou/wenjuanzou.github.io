When should assessment happen in the learning process?

April 2025, by Wenjuan (Joerica) Zou

Assessment shouldn't be tacked on at the end; it should be built into the whole learning journey. That's why I follow backward design: before developing any activity, clarify what learners need to achieve and what evidence will show they got there. Instead of "What content do I need to cover?", ask "How will I know they've learned it?"

Assessment for learning (formative)

Formative assessment happens during learning. It works like a GPS that helps learner and instructor course-correct in real time. You wouldn't build a bridge across a canyon and only check the structure when you reach the other side. I build in:

  • Quizzes and polls after short lessons
  • Retrieval practice, such as "What do you remember?" activities
  • Reflection prompts for self-assessment

Assessment of learning (summative)

Summative assessment is the final dress rehearsal: it measures what learners have mastered. In eLearning that might be a branching simulation that mirrors job tasks, a certification quiz aligned to objectives, or a final project reviewed by an instructor.

Why both matter

Using both isn't just about tracking progress; it improves learning itself. Well-designed formative checks support retention and motivation, and help learners reflect and recalibrate.

A design tip

Treat assessment as the heartbeat of the course. Build light-touch checkpoints along the way: a quick poll after a video, a knowledge check after each module, an exit ticket asking "What's one thing you'll apply tomorrow?"
