How do we create an effective framework for learners?

March 2025, by Wenjuan (Joerica) Zou

Designing learning isn't just delivering content; it's building a structure that helps learners grow. A strong framework should feel like a well-designed journey: clear, purposeful and responsive to who the learners are. These are the six components I rely on.

1. Start with clear learning objectives

Begin with the end in mind. Objectives should be specific, measurable and action-oriented, because vague goals lead to vague results. Think of it as setting the GPS before a road trip.

2. Understand the learners

Who are they? New to the topic or experienced? What misconceptions might they bring? In healthcare training, for example, tone and pacing differ for frontline staff and clinical educators.

3. Identify and address learning gaps

When learners struggle, it's often because we assumed too much. Look for gaps in knowledge, motivation, environment or digital fluency. For a team that has never used cloud tools, software onboarding should start with foundational concepts before features.

4. Adapt for novices and experts

Build in optional support and stretch: examples, definitions and guided walkthroughs for beginners; scenario-based tasks that push deeper thinking for experienced learners.

5. Use scaffolding

You wouldn't teach someone to ride a bike by pushing them down a hill. Start with step-by-step guidance, then shift to open-ended tasks, reducing support as mastery grows.

6. Enhance retention

Information alone isn't enough. I design around chunking, retrieval and association: visual groupings, metaphors for abstract ideas, and mind maps learners build themselves.

This framework isn't a checklist; it's a mindset: meet learners where they are, guide them with purpose, and build a structure strong enough to support change.
