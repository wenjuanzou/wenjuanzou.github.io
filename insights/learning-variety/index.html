How can strategies create variety in the learning process?

May 2025 · Wenjuan (Joerica) Zou

Variety isn't only about keeping things interesting. It's about equity, flexibility and impact: people process, engage with and apply information in different ways.

1. Offer more than one way in

Universal Design for Learning asks for multiple means of representation and engagement. I pair audio explanations with visuals, or let learners choose between a video walkthrough and a step-by-step guide. The goal isn't everything for everyone; it's a path every learner can follow confidently.

2. Target different cognitive levels

Using Bloom's taxonomy like a climbing wall, I layer warm-ups such as quizzes and flashcards before analytical and creative challenges like branching scenarios and reflection prompts.

3. Build motivation through ownership

Drawing on self-determination theory, I add personalized options, embedded choice and visible progress, such as badges that signal growth. Even letting learners choose the format of a project changes how they show up.

4. Improve retention with spacing and retrieval

Learning is a series of refreshes, not a single download. Short quizzes, reflection moments and recaps at intervals keep the path through the forest from disappearing.

5. Promote real-world application

Skills stick when they're tied to real stakes. In healthcare training, for instance, interactive case studies can ground compliance rules in patient-care decisions.

6. Reduce cognitive overload

Clarity, not clutter: chunking, microlearning and visual pairings let learners spend their energy on meaning rather than decoding the delivery.

7. Scaffold based on readiness

Following Vygotsky, meet learners just beyond what they can do alone: guided walkthroughs before independent application, discussion before self-assessment.

Variety is responsible design. Diversifying how we engage, challenge and support learners makes learning more inclusive and more durable.
