01. The problem

Busy adults know what to do but can't start

Brain Hacks was my final project for Designing Educational Media at Harvard Extension. The audience: adult learners and professionals who struggle with procrastination and motivation, curious about brain science but with little background in it.

02. Research

What learners told us before I built anything

I created three personas (Marcus, fitness-focused; Elena, career-driven; Alex, creatively curious), then ran a focus group with 8 adult learners. Three findings shaped the design:

  • They preferred short, interactive, media-rich content.
  • They had little neuroscience knowledge but high curiosity.
  • They wanted practical application over theory.

03. Design

A three-part journey

1

Learn the brain

What dopamine is, how reward shapes motivation, and why it matters, told through an animated "Brain" narrator.

2

Boost it wisely

A drag-and-drop game and avatar scenarios where learners apply strategies to relatable goals.

Why: the focus group asked for practice over theory.

3

Make it stick

Twelve strategies and a personal action plan learners leave with.

04. Testing and iteration

What changed after usability testing

Four usability testers went through the Storyline prototype. They responded well to the color scheme, avatars and voiceover. The character stories felt less relatable than I intended, so I rewrote them. In follow-up, testers could recall strategies such as incremental goal-setting.

All four testers reported learning something new about motivation. With four people, that is a formative signal, not proof of impact.
