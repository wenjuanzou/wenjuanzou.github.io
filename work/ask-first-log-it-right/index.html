01 · The business problem

New reps lose deals in the first five minutes

New Account Executives rarely lose deals for lack of product knowledge. They lose them by pitching before they understand the buyer. When a buyer asks "So what does your company do?", a new rep starts a product tour and never learns why the buyer took the call.

The second gap shows up after the call. Whatever the rep did learn rarely reaches the CRM. Without the buyer's pain, its cost, a deadline and a dated next step on the record, a manager can't coach the deal or forecast it.

So the course had to fix two things at once: the conversation, and the record the conversation produces.

02 · What people needed to do differently

Seven behaviors, six on the call and one after it

I action-mapped the goal down to observable behaviors, using the SPICED discovery framework from Winning by Design, which is common in B2B software sales.

  • Start with the situation: find out why the buyer took the call today.
  • Quantify the pain: turn "it's slow" into time, volume or headcount.
  • Explore the impact: what the problem costs the business.
  • Find the critical event: a date that makes it urgent.
  • Map the decision: the steps and who signs.
  • Then pitch, and book a dated next step.
  • Log facts, not guesses, in a five-line note before the next meeting.

03 · Constraints

Short, realistic, and honest about the tool

  • About 20 minutes. New reps are in back-to-back onboarding; anything longer gets skipped.
  • HubSpot changes its labels often. The walkthrough teaches the intent of each step so it survives UI changes.
  • No real customer data. Northpeak, Brightloom and every character are fictional.

04 · Key decisions

Three choices that shaped the experience

1

Train the conversation and the record together

The notes a learner earns in the call simulation are the exact fields they log in HubSpot afterward. Good questions unlock notes; pitching early costs trust and leaves fields empty.

Why: separate "sales skills" and "CRM training" courses teach two habits that never connect. Here the CRM record is the consequence of the conversation.

2

Model, then practice, then transfer

Learners first read an expert call where every question is tagged with the SPICED field it fills. Then they play the rep in a branching Storyline call with Dana. Finally they read a new call with a different buyer, with the rep's private guesses mixed in, and write the record from scratch.

Why: worked examples lower the load for novices, and a fresh scenario checks whether the skill transfers instead of the answers being memorized.

3

Put the support where the work happens

Two one-page job aids (a discovery cheat sheet and a HubSpot quick reference), if-then plans for the next three calls, and a 30-day plan with weekly manager reviews.

Why: much of what reps learn fades without use. A reference at the moment of need and a manager checking real calls keep the habit alive.

The Storyline simulation tracks trust, best answers and unlocked notes in variables, so every ending reflects the choices the learner made. The course site itself is custom HTML, CSS and JavaScript on Cloudflare Pages, with progress tracking and a scored knowledge check.

06 · Measurement

An evaluation plan tied to the sales manager's numbers

~40% → 70%discovery calls logged with a complete SPICED note, by day 30
22% → 30%discovery-to-demo conversion for new reps, within one quarter
NoteThese targets come from a fictional stakeholder intake written for this concept project. They are an evaluation plan, not measured results.

Behavior is checked on real calls: in week 1 the manager reviews one recorded call against a four-line scorecard (questions, pain, critical event and decision, CRM note), then one call clip and one HubSpot record each week until day 30.

07 · What I'd do next

Test it with real reps

Pilot with a small cohort of new hires, compare their CRM completeness with the previous cohort, and listen to which simulation choices people get wrong most often. Those are the moments to rewrite first.
