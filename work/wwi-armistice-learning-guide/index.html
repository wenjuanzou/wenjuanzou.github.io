01. The challenge

Make an exhibit matter to a 12-year-old

For Designing Educational Media at Harvard Extension, our team responded to a museum curation challenge: an interactive learning guide for grades 6–12 based on the 11.11.18 Centennial Armistice exhibit at the National WWI Museum and Memorial. I led the instructional design decisions on our team.

02. Approach

Backward design across history, ELA and art

  • Pre-visit hook: students close their eyes and listen to a recording of the guns falling silent. "What did peace sound like in 1918?"
  • Virtual scavenger hunt: identify three key figures, analyze two primary sources on civilian impact, explore one lasting legacy.
  • Inquiry questions: for example, "Did the Armistice truly end the war?"
  • Post-visit creation: design a memorial as a poster, drawing, sculpture or digital prototype.

The guide includes objectives, essential questions, assessment options and modifications for English language learners and younger students.

03. What I learned

Emotion as a door into history

Sound and creative tasks did more to make history personal than any reading. The project also taught me to scaffold one activity for very different learners.
