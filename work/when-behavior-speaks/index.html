01. The problem

Pain that looks like behavior

Many autistic children can't verbally express internal pain. Instead they may self-injure, seek pressure or refuse food. These signs are often read as autism traits, and treatable gastrointestinal conditions go unrecognized.

02. Goals

What learners should do

  • Recognize behavioral cues that may indicate GI distress.
  • Tell autism traits apart from pain-related behaviors.
  • Refer early to the right clinician.
  • Reflect on what they'll watch for in their next encounter.

03. Key decisions

A simulated intake, not a lecture

1

Put the learner in the room

Learners observe a child named Joey during an intake session and weigh his behavior and his caregiver's input before deciding what to do.

Why: scenario practice in a low-risk space builds the observation habit the job requires.

2

Bring in expert thinking

Commentary draws on published work by pediatric gastroenterologist Dr. Timothy Buie, synthesized from his research and talks.

3

Reward evidence-informed choices

Learners earn Insight Points for well-reasoned decisions and end with a reflection and a downloadable reference guide.

04. Process

Research, design document, storyboard, build

I synthesized research from GI and autism experts, wrote a design document and storyboard, then built the 11-slide module in Storyline with graphics, audio, video and animation. The design used action mapping, Knowles' adult learning principles and Bloom's taxonomy (understand → apply → evaluate).
