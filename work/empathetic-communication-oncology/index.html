Practicing the hardest conversations before they happen

01 · The problem

Clinical skills get trained; hard conversations don't

Nurse onboarding focuses on clinical skills. Emotional communication often gets less attention, and new nurses can feel unprepared for intense moments with patients and families. I built this module over one weekend as a work sample for oncology nurse onboarding.

02 · Action mapping

Three moments new nurses dread

  • A silent or tearful patient after treatment.
  • A patient who asks, "Am I going to die?"
  • A family member upset about delays.

Each scenario has branching decision points and mentor pop-up feedback that explains what a response does to the patient's trust.

03 · Key decisions

Designing for emotional safety

1

Low stakes, real dialogue

Learners choose responses in realistic conversations and see how the patient reacts, before they face the moment on a unit.

Why: rehearsal in a safe space reduces freezing in the real one.

2

A mentor, not a grader

Feedback comes from an experienced nurse character who coaches the choice instead of marking it wrong.

3

Calm visual tone

A clean, quiet visual style that keeps attention on the conversation.

04 · Process

Research to storyboard in a weekend

I reviewed clinical empathy literature in oncology settings, mapped the scenarios, wrote a design document and storyboard, and built the module in Storyline, grounded in Knowles' adult learning principles, Bloom's taxonomy and situated learning.
