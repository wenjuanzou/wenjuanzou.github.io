01 · The problem

Ethics training stops at the policy list

Most AI ethics training is a list of do's and don'ts. Real workplace issues don't arrive that way. Evidence is scattered across tools and conversations, ownership is unclear (who can pause a system?), and teams disagree about what the real risk is.

The hard part isn't defining bias. It's deciding what to do next when the impact is real and the information is incomplete.

02 · Course design

From AI user to AI decision-maker in one week

I designed the full one-week, self-paced Canvas course, not just the simulation.

  • Modules 1–2: responsible-use foundations and repeatable AI workflows: prompting, iteration and quality checks.
  • Module 3: the AI Ethics Investigation Lab, a Storyline capstone built around a biased hiring-system incident.
  • Module 4: transfer, turning the experience into a personal plan and a reusable decision habit.

03 · Key decisions

How the investigation teaches judgment

1

Use authentic workplace artifacts

Learners investigate through emails, team chats and leadership notes, the same channels where a real incident would surface.

Why: anchoring practice in real work makes the skill easier to recognize on the job.

2

Let clarity be earned

Evidence is revealed step by step. Learners gather clues across channels, notice patterns such as biased data signals and missing stop-authority, and only then make a recommendation.

Why: progressive disclosure mirrors how incidents unfold and keeps the decision from being obvious too early.

3

Feedback as coaching

The final recommendation gets feedback that explains the tradeoffs of each option, not just right or wrong.

Why: in ethics there is rarely one correct answer, so the reasoning is what learners need to take away.

4

End with a framework they can reuse

The lab closes with a simple Pause-and-Check framework for evaluating AI use in any workplace tool.

Why: the scenario is one case; the framework is what transfers.

04 · My role and tools

End to end, with AI as an assistant

I designed the scenario, evidence trail and assessment logic, wrote all learner-facing copy and feedback, built the Storyline interactions with states, variables and triggers, and integrated the lab into the Canvas course.

ChatGPT and Claude helped me brainstorm scenarios and tighten feedback copy; ElevenLabs produced voiceover and sound; an image model produced some visuals. I made the final decisions on learning flow, tone and feedback logic.

05 · Reflection

Ethics as a decision habit

Workplace reality sits between "AI is amazing, just use it" and "AI is risky, follow the policy." People need to act with limited information while harm is possible. What I wanted learners to practice was judgment: noticing early signals, slowing down at the right moment without freezing progress, choosing a responsible next step that fits their authority, and explaining that decision in a way that builds trust.
